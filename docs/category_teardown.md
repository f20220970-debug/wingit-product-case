# Premium beauty in India: where buying breaks, and where an at-home concierge can win

**What this is.** A category teardown for Wingit, an at-home beauty concierge in Gurugram: advisors bring
premium makeup, skincare and fragrance to the customer, who tries with guidance and pays only for what they keep
(Inc42, Peak XV Surge). Wingit is newly launched, so instead of reviewing its app, this maps how premium
customers buy beauty today, which problems Wingit's model solves, and where the model itself is likely to strain.
Review quotes are paraphrased and are anecdotes; survey figures are cited.

## How a premium customer buys beauty today
| Stage | What happens today | Where it breaks | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discover | Instagram, creators, friends, brand counters in a few metro malls | Advice is generic or paid; good counters are few and far | Founder's thesis: platforms aren't personalised (Surge profile) |
| Decide | Product pages, swatches, reviews | **Shade and finish look different on screen than in life** | Trustpilot: lipstick arrived a much lighter shade than pictured |
| Buy | Marketplace or beauty app | **Authenticity worry** | LocalCircles survey (23,000+ responses): 40% of buyers found a fake product in 3 years; 26% had an allergic reaction |
| Try | At home, after paying | Wrong shade or reaction is discovered only after money is spent | Same survey |
| Return | Ask for a return | **Opened beauty products are hard to return**; claims rejected as "used" | Trustpilot reviews; founder's thesis: today's platforms don't accept returns |
| Reorder | Search the app again | No memory of what suited you; repeat purchase starts from zero | Category pattern |

**The core problem:** beauty is bought online without the two things that make it safe in a store: trying it on
your own skin, and trusted advice. Customers carry all the risk (wrong shade, fakes, reactions, refused returns).

## What Wingit's model fixes
1. **Try before you pay** removes shade and fit risk: the customer only pays for what worked on their skin.
2. **Advisor-led, authentic stock** answers the counterfeit and "who do I trust" problems in one visit.
3. **No-return problem disappears:** customers don't return what they never bought.

## Where the model will strain (and what to watch)
| Risk | Why it matters | Metric to own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visit economics | Every visit costs advisor time, travel and kit; low-conversion visits burn cash | Contribution per visit; visits per advisor per day |
| Kit relevance | If the advisor brings the wrong products for this customer, the visit fails however good the advisor is | **Kit hit rate**: share of tried items that are bought |
| Advisor consistency | The brand is only as good as the weakest advisor | Conversion and rating spread across advisors |
| Hygiene of testers | Premium customers will not try shared testers casually | Tester policy; complaint rate |
| Repeat purchase | Customers don't need a visit to rebuy their foundation | Share of repeat revenue that comes without a visit (app reorder) |
| Acquisition cost | A visit-based service needs high-intent customers | CAC by channel; first-visit conversion; payback |

## Opportunities, ranked
1. **Personalise the kit before the visit** (biggest lever on visit economics and conversion). See the PRD.
2. **Visit-free replenishment:** remember every product a customer kept and let them reorder in one tap.
3. **Advisor playbook and scoring:** coach low-converting advisors with patterns from the best ones.
